# Addendum to the analysis plan: additional robustness analyses

**Date:** 27 October 2026, the date of the commit that adds this file.

**Approval:** approved by all authors on 27 October 2026.

**Status:**
- Written after the analyses of version 1.0 of the paper were known, and before the analyses below were run.
- The commit that adds this file precedes the commits that add their code and results.
- No rejection rate of the new designs and no new replication of the regime-re-estimating bootstrap had been computed when this file was written.

## Purpose

The Monte Carlo study of the paper (analysis plan, P3-P5) raises three questions:

1. Is the size of the regime-re-estimating bootstrap (3% in 200 replications) stable with more replications?
2. Does the month-clustered wild bootstrap keep its size when GPRC has heavy tails and a few months carry much of the identifying variation, as in the data?
3. Does it keep its size when the common volatility shock is serially correlated?

The analyses assess robustness. They are not used to select a specification or a new primary inference procedure.

## Common settings

- **Base design:** cell 3 of the Monte Carlo study: variant c, δ = δ̂, β₃ = 0. The calibration is `stage2/results/mc/calibration.json`; the panel is unbalanced as in the data.
- **Regime:** two-state Markov-switching model with 3 starting values; smoothed P > 0.5.
- **Seeds:** 20261013 + 30,000 + r for r = 0, …, 999, as in cell 3.
  - In design H, the regime path and the volatility innovations are those of cell 3.
  - In design S, the regime path and all draws of cell 3 are kept, and only the dynamics of the common volatility shock change.
- **Inference settings:** as in the Monte Carlo study, with B = 399 for the bootstrap procedures.
- **Smoke test:** a run with 2 replications per design checks the code before the full runs. Its output is discarded.

## R1. Regime-re-estimating bootstrap with 1,000 replications

- **Algorithm:** as in the 200-replication experiment (analysis plan, P4):
  - moving-block bootstrap with 6-month blocks and B = 199;
  - the Markov-switching model is re-estimated in each draw with 3 starting values;
  - draws with fewer than 5 or more than T/2 stress months are skipped.
- **Replications 0-199** are the existing ones and are not recomputed.
- **Reproduction check:** before the run, replications 0-9 are recomputed and must equal the stored values. If they differ, all 1,000 replications are recomputed with the current code, and the old and new results for replications 0-199 are both reported.
- **Reported:**
  - number of replications used;
  - rejection rate at 5% with its Monte Carlo standard error and 95% Wilson interval;
  - rejection rate of DK (L = 4) on the same replications;
  - mean and median bootstrap standard error;
  - mean number of skipped draws.

## H. Heavy-tailed GPRC

- **Change:**
  - The GPRC innovations h_t and u_it are drawn from a Student-t distribution with ν degrees of freedom.
  - The draws are multiplied by sqrt((ν − 2)/ν), so their variance stays at 1; u_it is also multiplied by σ_u, as in cell 3.
  - All other parameters and the volatility innovations are unchanged. Nothing is recalibrated.
- **Choice of ν, before any rejection rate is computed:**
  - For each ν in {2.5, 3, 4, 5, 8}, 200 panels (r = 0-199) give the median share of the top 5 months in the sum of squares of the β₃ regressor.
  - The value of ν whose median is closest to the observed 0.528 is used.
  - Only this share and the excess kurtosis of GPRC are computed in this step.
- **Replications:** 1,000.

## S. Serially correlated common volatility shock (what-if design)

- **Change:**
  - f_t = 0.5 f_{t−1} + sqrt(0.75) ε_t, where ε_t is the standard normal draw of cell 3, so the variance of f_t stays at 1.
  - GPRC and all other parameters are unchanged.
  - ρ = 0.5 is fixed, not calibrated, because the observed residuals show no lag-1 autocorrelation.
- **Replications:** 1,000.

## Outcomes for H and S

- Rejection rates at 5% for OLS, each with its Monte Carlo standard error and 95% Wilson interval, for these procedures:
  - DK with L = 4;
  - DK fixed-b;
  - HC3;
  - clustering by month;
  - Webb wild cluster bootstrap by country;
  - wild bootstrap by month;
  - moving-block bootstrap.
- Mean β̂₃, and the share of replications with β̂₃ ≤ −0.171.
- The diagnostics in the table above, to show how close each design comes to the data.

## Decision rules

- The month-clustered wild bootstrap remains the primary inference procedure.
- **R1:** the size is described as follows:
  - below nominal if the upper bound of the 95% interval is below 5%;
  - close to nominal if the interval contains 5%;
  - above nominal if the lower bound exceeds 5%.
- **H and S:**
  - A procedure that rejects in more than 7% of the replications of a design is described as oversized under that design. The 7% is the tolerance of the original selection rule (P3).
  - If the month-clustered wild bootstrap is oversized under H or S, the paper describes its p-value of about 0.14 as a lower bound. It then gives more weight to the procedures that keep their size under that design, also in the abstract.
- All results are reported in this repository and summarised in the paper, whatever their direction.
